9.1

The Signed Area Concept

What a Definite Integral Measures from a Graph

The definite integral ∫ab f(x) dx gives the net signed area between the curve y = f(x) and the x-axis on [a, b].

  • Above x-axis (f(x) > 0): area counts as positive
  • Below x-axis (f(x) < 0): area counts as negative

Net area means regions below the x-axis cancel out regions above. Total area means you count all regions as positive (use absolute value).

Net Area vs Total Area
Net Area (Signed)
∫ab f(x) dx

Above − Below

Regions below x-axis subtract from the total. Cancellation happens.

Example: If upper region = 6 and lower region = 4,
net area = 6 − 4 = 2
Total Area (Unsigned)
∫ab |f(x)| dx

Above + Below (both positive)

No cancellation. Every region adds to the total.

Example: If upper region = 6 and lower region = 4,
total area = 6 + 4 = 10
⚠️ FRQ language matters: "Find the net area" → use the signed integral directly. "Find the total area" → split at x-intercepts and add absolute values of each piece. Confusing these costs points.
Green region is above x-axis (+6). Red region is below x-axis (−4). Net area = 2. Total area = 10.
9.2

Geometry Formulas for Reading Graphs

The only formulas you need
When the Graph Is Made of Geometric Shapes

On the AP exam, piecewise graphs are frequently drawn using line segments, triangles, rectangles, and semicircles. Instead of antidifferentiating, you use geometry formulas directly.

Rectangle
A = base × height
Triangle
A = ½ × base × height
Semicircle
A = ½ × π × r²
Trapezoid
A = ½(b₁ + b₂) × h
Right Triangle
A = ½ × base × height
Sign Rule
Subtract if below x-axis
💡 Semicircle radius: If the graph shows a semicircle sitting on the x-axis, the diameter is the base width. Radius = diameter / 2. For a semicircle above the x-axis: +½πr². Below: −½πr².
9.3

Graphs Made of Line Segments

How to Read Them

When the graph of f is made entirely of straight line segments, every region between the graph and the x-axis is a triangle, rectangle, or trapezoid. Identify each shape, compute its area, then apply the sign (+ above, − below).

  1. Identify where the graph crosses the x-axis (changes from above to below or vice versa).→ These are the boundaries between positive and negative regions.
  2. Identify each shape in each region.→ Triangle, rectangle, or trapezoid? Read height and base from the graph.
  3. Compute each area using the geometry formula.
  4. Assign signs: + if above x-axis, − if below.
  5. Sum all signed areas for the net area, or sum absolute values for total area.
Triangle A₁ (base=2, height=2) above axis · Rectangle A₂ (width=1, height=1) above axis · Triangle A₃ (base=2, height=2) below axis
Practice · Line Segment Graphs
Problem
Using the graph above: Find (a) ∫₀⁵ f(x) dx and (b) the total area from x=0 to x=5.
1
Identify each region and its shape
A₁ = Triangle above x-axis on [0,2]: base = 2, height = 2
A₂ = Rectangle above x-axis on [2,3]: width = 1, height = 1
A₃ = Triangle below x-axis on [3,5]: base = 2, height = 2
2
Compute each geometric area
A₁ = ½ × 2 × 2 = 2
A₂ = 1 × 1 = 1
A₃ = ½ × 2 × 2 = 2
3
Apply signs (+ above, − below)
A₁ = +2   A₂ = +1   A₃ = −2
4
Net area (a) and Total area (b)
(a) ∫₀⁵ f(x) dx = 2 + 1 + (−2) = 1
(b) Total area = |2| + |1| + |−2| = 2 + 1 + 2 = 5
Problem
A graph of f consists of: a triangle above the x-axis from x=0 to x=3 with height 4, then a triangle below the x-axis from x=3 to x=5 with height 3. Find the net and total area on [0,5].
Step 1 — Compute the area of the triangle above the axis (base=3, height=4)
Step 2 — Compute the area of the triangle below the axis (base=2, height=3)
Step 3 — Net area: apply signs and sum
Step 4 — Total area: sum absolute values
A₁ = ½ × 3 × 4 = 6
A₂ = ½ × 2 × 3 = 3

Net area: 6 + (−3) = 3
Total area: 6 + 3 = 9
Problem
The graph of g consists of a trapezoid above the axis from x=0 to x=4 (parallel sides of length 3 and 5, height 4), and a rectangle below the axis from x=4 to x=6 (width=2, height=2). Find ∫₀⁶ g(x) dx and the total area.
Trapezoid: A₁ = ½(3+5)×4 = ½(8)(4) = 16 (above, positive)
Rectangle: A₂ = 2×2 = 4 (below, negative)

Net: ∫₀⁶ g(x) dx = 16 + (−4) = 12
Total area = 16 + 4 = 20
9.4

Graphs with Semicircles

The most tested special shape on the AP exam
Finding the Radius from a Graph

When you see a semicircular arc, the diameter = the base width of the arc along the x-axis. Radius = diameter ÷ 2.

Semicircle Area Formula
Area = 12πr²

Above x-axis → add the area (positive)
Below x-axis → subtract the area (negative)

If the diameter spans from x = a to x = b:   r = b − a2
Semicircle above axis (r=2): area = 2π. Flat segment: area = 0. Semicircle below axis (r=1): area = −π/2.
∫₀⁸ f(x) dx = 2π + 0 − π/2 = 3π/2
Practice · Semicircle Graphs
Problem
The graph of f consists of a semicircle above the x-axis from x=0 to x=6 (diameter=6), and a right triangle below the x-axis from x=6 to x=10 (base=4, height=3). Find ∫₀¹⁰ f(x) dx.
1
Find the radius of the semicircle
Diameter = 6 → r = 6/2 = 3
2
Compute semicircle area (above = positive)
A₁ = ½π(3²) = ½π(9) = 9π/2
3
Compute triangle area (below = negative)
A₂ = ½ × 4 × 3 = 6 → signed: −6
4
Sum for net integral
∫₀¹⁰ f(x) dx = 9π/2 + (−6) = 9π/2 − 6
Problem
The graph of g shows a semicircle below the x-axis from x=0 to x=4 (diameter=4), then a triangle above the x-axis from x=4 to x=7 (base=3, height=4). Find ∫₀⁷ g(x) dx and the total area.
Step 1 — Find the radius of the semicircle. Compute its area. Apply sign.
Step 2 — Compute triangle area. Apply sign.
Step 3 — Net integral
Step 4 — Total area (add absolute values)
r = 2  |  A₁ = ½π(4) = 2π → signed: −2π
A₂ = ½(3)(4) = 6 → signed: +6

Net: ∫₀⁷ g(x) dx = −2π + 6 = 6 − 2π
Total: 2π + 6 = 2π + 6
Problem
The graph of h from x=0 to x=10 consists of: a semicircle above the axis from x=0 to x=4 (diameter=4), a rectangle above the axis from x=4 to x=6 (height=2), and a semicircle below the axis from x=6 to x=10 (diameter=4). Find ∫₀¹⁰ h(x) dx.
Semicircle 1: r=2, above → A₁ = +½π(4) = +2π
Rectangle: width=2, height=2 → A₂ = +4
Semicircle 2: r=2, below → A₃ = −½π(4) = −2π

∫₀¹⁰ h(x) dx = 2π + 4 − 2π = 4
9.5

Applying Integral Properties to Graph Problems

The 5 Properties You Need
PropertyStatementWhen to Use
Flip bounds ∫ab f = −∫ba f Asked for integral in reverse direction
Same bounds ∫aa f = 0 Upper = lower limit
Split interval ∫ab f = ∫ac f + ∫cb f Given pieces, asked for total
Scalar ∫ab k·f = k·∫ab f Constant multiple of a known integral
Sum/Diff ∫[f ± g] = ∫f ± ∫g Both f and g are known from graph
Practice · Integral Properties from Graph Data
Problem
A graph of f gives: ∫₀³ f(x) dx = 5, ∫₃⁷ f(x) dx = −2, ∫₀⁷ g(x) dx = 8. Find:
(a) ∫₀⁷ f(x) dx   (b) ∫₇³ f(x) dx   (c) ∫₀⁷ [2f(x) + g(x)] dx
1
(a) Split interval property
∫₀⁷ f = ∫₀³ f + ∫₃⁷ f = 5 + (−2) = 3
→ The interval [0,7] = [0,3] ∪ [3,7]. Just add the two known pieces.
2
(b) Flip bounds — reverse direction
∫₇³ f = −∫₃⁷ f = −(−2) = 2
→ Swapping upper and lower bounds negates the integral.
3
(c) Scalar and sum properties
∫₀⁷ [2f + g] = 2·∫₀⁷ f + ∫₀⁷ g = 2(3) + 8 = 6 + 8 = 14
→ Split across addition, factor out constant 2, then use known values.
Problem
Given: ∫₁⁵ f(x) dx = 12, ∫₅⁹ f(x) dx = −4. Find:
(a) ∫₁⁹ f(x) dx   (b) ∫₉⁵ f(x) dx   (c) ∫₁⁹ 3f(x) dx
(a) Add the two pieces using the split interval property
(b) Use the flip bounds property on ∫₅⁹ f
(c) Factor out 3 using the scalar property, use (a)
(a) 12 + (−4) = 8
(b) −(−4) = 4
(c) 3 × 8 = 24
Problem
From a graph you read: ∫₀⁴ f(x) dx = 7, ∫₄⁸ f(x) dx = −3, ∫₀⁸ g(x) dx = 10.
Find: (a) ∫₈⁰ f(x) dx   (b) ∫₀⁸ [f(x) − 2g(x)] dx   (c) ∫₄⁰ f(x) dx
∫₀⁸ f = 7 + (−3) = 4

(a) ∫₈⁰ f = −∫₀⁸ f = −4
(b) ∫₀⁸ [f − 2g] = ∫₀⁸ f − 2∫₀⁸ g = 4 − 2(10) = 4 − 20 = −16
(c) ∫₄⁰ f = −∫₀⁴ f = −7
9.6

Mixed Graph — The Classic FRQ Type

Semicircle + line segments combined
What to Expect on the AP Exam

One of the most common FRQ graph problems gives a piecewise function made of a semicircle and several line segments combined. You must identify each piece, compute each area, and apply the correct sign.

ℹ️ Standard approach: Break the graph into its individual shapes. Never try to compute one big area at once. Label each piece with its shape, dimensions, area, and sign — then sum.
Three regions: semicircle above (r=1) · triangle above (b=2,h=2) · triangle below (b=1,h=1)
∫₀⁵ f(x) dx = π/2 + 2 − ½ = π/2 + 3/2
Practice · Mixed Graphs (FRQ Style)
Problem — Use the mixed graph above
Find: (a) ∫₀⁵ f(x) dx  (b) total area on [0,5]  (c) ∫₅⁰ f(x) dx
1
List all regions with shapes, dimensions, and areas
Semicircle [0,2]: r=1, above → area = ½π(1)² = π/2, signed: +π/2
Triangle [2,4]: base=2, height=2, above → area = ½(2)(2) = 2, signed: +2
Triangle [4,5]: base=1, height=1, below → area = ½(1)(1) = ½, signed: −½
2
(a) Net integral
∫₀⁵ f(x) dx = π/2 + 2 − ½ = π/2 + 3/2
3
(b) Total area
|π/2| + |2| + |½| = π/2 + 2 + ½ = π/2 + 5/2
4
(c) Flip bounds property
∫₅⁰ f(x) dx = −∫₀⁵ f(x) dx = −(π/2 + 3/2)
Problem
A graph of h consists of: a semicircle above the x-axis from x=0 to x=4 (r=2), a horizontal line segment along the x-axis from x=4 to x=6, and a triangle below the x-axis from x=6 to x=9 (base=3, height=2). Find ∫₀⁹ h(x) dx and total area.
Semicircle: r=2, above. Area = ½πr² = ... Signed = ...
Horizontal segment along x-axis: area = ?
Triangle: base=3, height=2, below. Area = ½bh = ... Signed = ...
Net integral and total area
A₁ = ½π(4) = 2π → signed: +2π
A₂ = 0 (on x-axis, no area)
A₃ = ½(3)(2) = 3 → signed: −3

Net: ∫₀⁹ h(x) dx = 2π + 0 − 3 = 2π − 3
Total area = 2π + 0 + 3 = 2π + 3
Problem
A graph of k consists of: a right triangle above the axis from x=0 to x=3 (base=3, height=4), a semicircle below the axis from x=3 to x=7 (diameter=4), and a rectangle above the axis from x=7 to x=9 (width=2, height=3). Find ∫₀⁹ k(x) dx, total area, and ∫₉⁷ k(x) dx.
Triangle [0,3]: ½(3)(4) = 6 → +6
Semicircle [3,7]: r=2, below → −½π(4) = −2π
Rectangle [7,9]: 2×3 = 6 → +6

Net: ∫₀⁹ k(x) dx = 6 − 2π + 6 = 12 − 2π
Total area = 6 + 2π + 6 = 12 + 2π

∫₉⁷ k(x) dx = −∫₇⁹ k(x) dx = −6 = −6
